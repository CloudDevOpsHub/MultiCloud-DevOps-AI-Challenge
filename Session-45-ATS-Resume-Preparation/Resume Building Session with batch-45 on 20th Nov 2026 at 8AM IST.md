## Decisions Made

- **Resume, not CV:** Participants applying for jobs must create a **resume** (max 2 pages, ideally 1); a CV is only for higher education or academic pursuits such as a PhD or Master's abroad. 
- **Latest experience first:** The most recent company and Cloud/DevOps-related work must appear on the **first page**, ideally within the first half-page, as package/salary is tied to the last company. 
- **Commercial vs. Real resume:** Maintain a **real resume** (genuine skills only) and a **commercial resume** tailored per job description using AI-extracted keywords — never blindly copy-paste someone else's resume. 
- **ATS keyword alignment is mandatory:** Every resume must include keywords matching the target job description; missing keywords cause automatic rejection before any human review. 
- **PDF format required:** Resumes must be submitted in PDF format, named with the candidate's full name. 
- **No generic objective statements:** Replace generic objectives with a crisp, tailored professional summary aligned to the specific role.

## Core Concepts Covered

### CV vs. Resume

- **CV (Curriculum Vitae):** A detailed document used for higher education applications (PhD, Master's abroad); not appropriate for job applications. 
- **Resume:** A concise professional document (max 2 pages) used exclusively for job applications; must focus only on professional experience and relevant projects. 
- Common mistake flagged: many participants had submitted CVs when applying for jobs, leading to immediate rejection. 

### How ATS Works

- **ATS (Applicant Tracking System):** Software used by companies to automatically scan, rank, and shortlist resumes based on keyword matching against the job description. 
- Recruiters spend only **3 seconds** on initial resume review; everything is automated first. 
- Out of 100 applicants, ATS ranks resumes in order; HR typically only reviews the **top 10–20**. 
- ATS rejects resumes with **wrong spellings** (e.g., "Jenkins" misspelled), **incorrect capitalization** (e.g., "cicd" instead of "CICD"), or **missing keywords**. 
- ATS may treat **Amazon** and **AWS** as different keywords — both must be written in the resume if applicable. 

### Why Calls Are Not Coming

- Primary reasons identified: resume not ATS-optimized, missing Cloud/DevOps keywords, inactive LinkedIn profile, and lack of GitHub activity. 
- Recruiters also verify LinkedIn and GitHub profiles; a profile with no activity or "greenery" (GitHub contribution graph) signals inactivity.

## Resume Format & Structure

### Mandatory Sections (in order)

1. **Full name** (not initials or nicknames) — HR expects a full professional name. 
2. **Contact details:** Personal email (with full name, not "angel_priya123"), mobile number with **+91 prefix**, LinkedIn URL (custom, not default). 
3. **Professional summary / objective:** 2–3 crisp lines; must be role-specific and updated per application. 
4. **Relevant work experience:** Latest company first; Cloud/DevOps responsibilities must be highlighted here. 
5. **Projects:** With company name, your role, duration (month/year), and quantified outcomes. 
6. **Skills:** Listed using correct capitalization (AWS, S3, CICD, Jenkins, Docker, Kubernetes). 
7. **Certifications:** Any certificate — even Microsoft Office or Excel — adds value if it mentions a recognizable brand. 
8. **Education:** Mentioned but not the focus; recent graduates (2024–2026 batch) should not over-emphasize academics. 

### Formatting Rules

- **No photo** unless you have a genuinely professional one; avoid adding it by default. 
- **No home address** — write only the nearest metro/large city; do not write hometown or village name. 
- **No unnecessary details:** Avoid mentioning 10-year-old technologies, irrelevant hobbies, or outdated skills. 
- **Consistent formatting:** ATS can reject resumes with inconsistent fonts, incorrect capitalization, or two-column layouts (some ATS tools read line-by-line and may misparse columns). 
- **No pink, green, or black backgrounds** — keep it clean, black-and-white, professional.

## Keyword Strategy

### Critical Keywords for Cloud/DevOps Resumes (2–6 years experience)

- **Cloud platforms:** AWS / Amazon Web Services (both variants), Google Cloud / GCP, Azure, Hybrid Cloud, Multi-Cloud. 
- **Core tools:** Docker, Kubernetes, Jenkins, Terraform, Ansible, Git/GitHub. 
- **Practices:** CI/CD, DevSecOps, Continuous Monitoring, Continuous Testing, Infrastructure as Code. 
- **Linux/Unix:** Write both "Linux" and "Unix" together. 
- **Security tools** (if DevSecOps): Trivy, SonarQube. 
- **Cloud service models:** IaaS, PaaS, SaaS — include these if cloud projects are mentioned. 
- **AI-adjacent keywords** (emerging): AIOps, MLOps, MCP Server, Prompt Engineering — add 1–2 points if relevant. 

### Keyword Placement Rules

- Keywords must appear in **experience bullets and project descriptions**, not just the skills section. 
- Use **action-oriented, strong verbs**: "Designed," "Deployed," "Automated," "Architected," "Reduced," "Optimized" — avoid starting lines with "Support" or "Assisted." 
- Add **quantified metrics** wherever possible: uptime percentage, deployment time reduction, cost savings, number of servers managed. 
- For experienced candidates (5+ years): include words like **"Mentored," "Managed," "Led,"** to signal seniority. 

### Handling Tool Gaps

- If a JD requires a tool you haven't used (e.g., Datadog) but you know a similar one (e.g., Prometheus/Grafana): write the required tool in the resume and be prepared to explain your adjacent expertise in the interview. 
- You must be able to justify every metric and keyword you write — interviewers will cross-question every number.

- Submitted **CVs instead of resumes** — one participant's document was immediately flagged as a CV (academic format).

- **LinkedIn URL missing or non-custom:** HR expects a professional LinkedIn link.

## LinkedIn & GitHub Optimization

### LinkedIn Activity

- LinkedIn uses a **machine learning algorithm** to rank profiles; active profiles appear higher in recruiter searches. 
- Update your Naukri profile **twice daily** if calls are not coming — the algorithm rewards freshness. 
- Top 15–20 active profiles on the leaderboard are getting calls; inactive profiles are being ignored by recruiters. 
- Post content regularly: use **5–10 relevant hashtags** per post (not more). 
- **Repost and engage** with community content — foreign professionals actively help each other; Indian professionals should do the same. 

### LinkedIn Profile Tips

- Check **Search Appearances** in LinkedIn analytics to see which keywords recruiters are using to find you — add those keywords to your resume. 
- Check **Recruiter Actions** to see how many times your profile appeared in searches. 
- **Photo:** Use a professional photo if available; do not add a casual or low-quality photo. 
- Do not write your hometown — write the nearest major city; write "Open to Relocate [City]" if applicable. 

### GitHub Activity

- GitHub "greenery" (contribution graph) is checked by recruiters; consistent commits signal active learning. 
- Fork the class repository, make changes, raise PRs, and become a **contributor** — this builds a verifiable public profile. 
- Copy session notes into your own GitHub repo (README files) to demonstrate continuous learning. 
- Vikas is the **CNCF Indore organizer** (Central India) — joining and contributing to community repos adds credibility.

## AI Tools for Resume Optimization

### Recommended Workflow

1. **Create the resume manually first** (2 days) — print it, mark corrections with pencil, read it 4+ times. 
2. Once the manual draft is ready, use **ChatGPT or Claude** with structured prompts to tailor it. 
3. **Prompt sequence suggested:**
    - Prompt 1: Paste the JD → ask AI to extract required skills, keywords, and recommended additions. 
    - Prompt 2: Ask AI to rewrite professional summary aligned to the JD. 
    - Prompt 3: Ask AI to rewrite bullet points in **STAR format** with action verbs. 
    - Prompt 4: Ask AI to generate **50–100 interview questions** based on the JD and your resume. 
    - Prompt 5: Ask AI to generate a **full application package** (resume + cover letter + Q&A). 
4. **Always review AI output** — read every change before accepting; AI may insert inaccurate claims. 
5. A future session will cover building a **custom ATS application** using Python on GCP with AI to score resumes against JDs. 

### Cover Letter Guidance

- Cover letters are **not attached as a file** — they go in the **email body** when applying directly. 
- Subject line of the email is more important than the cover letter itself. 
- Only required when explicitly requested (more common for foreign/international applications).

## Professional Behavior & Interview Preparation

### Introduction (Tell Me About Yourself)

- Keep it **crisp and direct**: Name → total years of experience → current/last role → key expertise areas. 
- Do not list all AWS services in the introduction — keep it high-level. 
- Prepare for improvement across every telling: each version should be slightly better. 

### Interview Do's and Don'ts

- **Never badmouth** your current or previous manager/company — interviewers will reject you for it. 
- **Be professional throughout** — excessive familiarity or casualness can lead to rejection. 
- When asked "Why are you leaving?", never mention personal conflicts with management. 
- **Focus on one strong story** per competency area; do not dump everything in the introduction. 
- Be specific to your domain: Cloud engineers should stay on cloud topics; don't drift into unrelated areas. 

### Day-to-Day Activities to Prepare (for experience candidates)

- Stand-up calls: what you did yesterday, today's plan, blockers. 
- Jira/Azure DevOps board: picking tickets by priority, sprint planning. 
- PR reviews, whitelisting approvals, client coordination. 
- Vikas will cover **5–7 day-to-day activity points** per technology (Linux, AWS, Docker, Kubernetes) in the next class.

## 30 Possible Resume-Building Questions and Answers

These questions and answers are based on the resume-building session. They are written as practical questions a candidate may ask while preparing or updating a resume for Cloud and DevOps roles.

### 1. Should I create a CV or a resume when applying for a job?

**Answer:** Create a resume for job applications. Keep it concise, ideally one page and no more than two pages. A CV is generally used for academic or higher-education applications, such as a PhD or Master's program abroad.

### 2. How many pages should my resume have?

**Answer:** Keep your resume to a maximum of two pages. Ideally, make it one page if you can present your relevant experience and skills clearly without removing important information.

### 3. Which experience should I show first in my resume?

**Answer:** Follow reverse chronological order: show your latest company and latest role first. Keep your recent Cloud or DevOps-related responsibilities on the first page, preferably in the first half-page, so recruiters can quickly see your current experience.

### 4. Should I use the same resume for every job application?

**Answer:** Maintain two versions:
- **Real resume:** Your genuine skills, experience, and projects.
- **Commercial resume:** A version tailored to a particular job description using relevant keywords.

Customize the commercial resume for the role, but never add skills or experience you do not genuinely have.

### 5. What is ATS, and why is it important?

**Answer:** ATS stands for Applicant Tracking System. It is software companies use to scan, rank, and shortlist resumes by comparing them with a job description. Missing relevant keywords, spelling errors, and formatting problems can reduce the chance of your resume reaching a recruiter.

### 6. How does ATS compare my resume with a job description?

**Answer:** ATS looks for relevant terms and keywords from the job description in your resume. For example, if the role asks for AWS, Kubernetes, Terraform, and CI/CD, those terms should appear where they accurately describe your skills, experience, and projects—not only in a standalone skills list.

### 7. Should I write AWS or Amazon Web Services in my resume?

**Answer:** If you have relevant experience, include both **AWS** and **Amazon Web Services**. The session highlighted that an ATS may treat these as different keywords, so including both forms can help keyword alignment.

### 8. What sections should my resume contain, and in what order?

**Answer:** Use the following order from the session:
1. Full professional name
2. Contact details
3. Professional summary
4. Relevant work experience
5. Projects
6. Skills
7. Certifications
8. Education

Keep the most relevant professional information easy to find.

### 9. What should I include in my contact details?

**Answer:** Include a professional email address, mobile number with the `+91` prefix, and a customized LinkedIn URL. Use your full professional name rather than a casual email address or nickname.

### 10. Should I add my photograph and complete home address?

**Answer:** Do not add a photograph by default. Add one only if you have a genuinely professional photo and it is appropriate for the application. Do not include your full home address; mention the nearest major city instead.

### 11. How should I write my professional summary?

**Answer:** Write a crisp, role-specific summary in two to three lines. Mention your total experience, current or last role, and key expertise areas. Tailor it to the target job instead of using a generic objective statement.

### 12. Why should I avoid a generic career objective?

**Answer:** A generic objective does not show why your experience matches the job. Replace it with a professional summary that directly reflects the target role and the relevant skills you can demonstrate.

### 13. I am a software developer moving towards DevOps. How should I position my resume?

**Answer:** Highlight the DevOps work you genuinely performed as part of your developer role, such as CI/CD activities or relevant cloud work. Position yourself accurately—for example, as a developer with DevOps skills—rather than claiming experience you do not have. Align the headline with the role you are targeting when your experience supports it.

### 14. I have a non-Cloud/DevOps job title. Should I change it on my resume?

**Answer:** Your headline can reflect the role you are targeting, provided it is supported by your actual skills and experience. For example, a candidate moving from .NET development towards DevOps can reduce unrelated technology emphasis and highlight genuine Cloud/DevOps responsibilities. Do not misrepresent your official employment title or work history.

### 15. What should I write in the project section?

**Answer:** For each relevant project, include the company name, your role, project duration in month/year format, the work you performed, technologies used, and measurable outcomes where available. Prioritize Cloud and DevOps projects relevant to the target job.

### 16. I do not have a separate Cloud or DevOps project at work. What should I do?

**Answer:** Identify and describe genuine Cloud/DevOps activities you performed in your existing role. The session emphasized that missing or generic project sections weaken a resume. Do not invent a project or claim production experience you did not have.

### 17. How should I write my work-experience bullet points?

**Answer:** Use action-oriented verbs such as **Designed, Deployed, Automated, Architected, Reduced,** and **Optimized**. Explain what you did, which tools or technologies you used, and what result you achieved. Avoid starting every bullet with vague words such as "Support" or "Assisted."

### 18. Should I include numbers and metrics in my project descriptions?

**Answer:** Yes, wherever you have genuine and verifiable figures. Examples include uptime, deployment-time reduction, cost savings, and the number of servers managed. Be ready to explain how every number was measured because interviewers may question your metrics.

### 19. Which Cloud and DevOps keywords should I consider adding?

**Answer:** Add the keywords that genuinely match your skills and the target job description. The session covered:
- Cloud: AWS, Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud, GCP, Azure, Hybrid Cloud, Multi-Cloud
- Tools: Docker, Kubernetes, Jenkins, Terraform, Ansible, Git, GitHub
- Practices: CI/CD, DevSecOps, Continuous Monitoring, Continuous Testing, Infrastructure as Code
- Operating systems: Linux and Unix
- Security: Trivy and SonarQube, where relevant
- Cloud service models: IaaS, PaaS, SaaS
- Emerging areas: AIOps, MLOps, MCP Server, Prompt Engineering, where relevant

### 20. Is it enough to put all keywords in the skills section?

**Answer:** No. The session emphasized that keywords should also appear naturally in your experience bullets and project descriptions. Show how you used a tool or practice, not just that you know its name.

### 21. I have used Prometheus and Grafana, but the job description asks for Datadog. What should I do?

**Answer:** Do not claim hands-on Datadog experience if you have not used it. You can highlight your genuine Prometheus and Grafana experience as adjacent monitoring expertise and prepare to explain how those skills relate to the monitoring requirements in the job description.

### 22. Can I add a tool to my resume just because it appears in the job description?

**Answer:** No. Include a keyword only when it accurately represents your experience, knowledge, or relevant hands-on exposure. The session stressed that you must be able to justify every keyword and metric during an interview.

### 23. What should a fresher include in a Cloud/DevOps resume?

**Answer:** Focus on relevant hands-on knowledge, internships, practical projects, tools, certifications, and learning activities. Use honest wording such as "hands-on knowledge in" when you are still developing experience. Avoid unrelated academic details that do not support the target role.

### 24. I have five or more years of experience. How can I show seniority?

**Answer:** Where accurate, use words such as **Mentored, Managed,** and **Led** to describe your responsibilities. Add genuine project outcomes and measurable results to demonstrate ownership and impact.

### 25. What formatting should I use to make my resume ATS-friendly?

**Answer:** Keep the layout clean and consistent. Use readable fonts, correct capitalization, and a simple structure. Avoid two-column layouts because some ATS tools may read content line by line and misinterpret columns. The session recommended a professional black-and-white presentation without colored backgrounds.

### 26. Should I submit my resume in Word or PDF format, and how should I name it?

**Answer:** The session instructed participants to submit resumes in PDF format. Name the PDF using your full professional name so it is easy for recruiters to identify.

### 27. Can I use ChatGPT or Claude to create my resume?

**Answer:** Yes, but first create your own genuine resume manually. Then use ChatGPT or Claude to extract job-description keywords, improve your summary, rewrite bullets in STAR format, and prepare interview questions. Review every suggested change before accepting it because AI can introduce inaccurate claims.

### 28. What is the recommended AI prompt sequence for tailoring my resume?

**Answer:** Follow the sequence covered in the session:
1. Paste the job description and ask AI to extract required skills, keywords, and recommended additions.
2. Ask it to rewrite your professional summary for that job.
3. Ask it to rewrite your bullet points in STAR format using action verbs.
4. Ask it to generate 50–100 interview questions based on the job description and your resume.
5. Ask it to prepare an application package with the resume, cover letter, and interview Q&A.

Always verify the output against your real experience.

### 29. Should I attach a cover letter as a separate file when applying by email?

**Answer:** According to the session, when applying directly by email, put the cover letter in the email body rather than attaching it as a separate file. A cover letter is needed when explicitly requested and is more common in some foreign or international applications. Pay attention to the email subject line.

### 30. My resume is ready, but I am not getting interview calls. What should I review?

**Answer:** Review more than the document itself:
- Check whether the resume is ATS-aligned with the target job description.
- Confirm that relevant Cloud/DevOps keywords appear in your experience and projects.
- Check spelling, capitalization, formatting, and PDF output.
- Make sure your LinkedIn URL and profile are professional and active.
- Keep your GitHub profile active by contributing to repositories, updating notes, and raising pull requests.
- Review LinkedIn Search Appearances and recruiter-related analytics to understand which keywords are helping people find your profile.
- Ensure your resume headline and summary match the role you are targeting.

The session also recommended regular LinkedIn engagement and relevant hashtags, while keeping the resume itself factual and role-focused.

## Resume-Building Practice Checklist

Use this checklist after preparing your first resume draft:

- [ ] I have created a resume, not an academic CV.
- [ ] My resume is no longer than two pages.
- [ ] My latest relevant experience appears first.
- [ ] My full professional name and contact details are correct.
- [ ] My LinkedIn URL is customized and included.
- [ ] My professional summary is specific to the target role.
- [ ] My projects include my role, company, duration, tools, and outcomes.
- [ ] My Cloud/DevOps keywords match the job description and my actual experience.
- [ ] Keywords appear in experience and project bullets, not only in the skills section.
- [ ] My bullets use action verbs and genuine metrics where possible.
- [ ] My spelling, capitalization, fonts, and layout are consistent.
- [ ] I have removed irrelevant or outdated details.
- [ ] I have reviewed every AI-generated change and removed unsupported claims.
- [ ] I have saved the final resume as a PDF using my full name.
- [ ] My LinkedIn and GitHub profiles show relevant professional activity.
